# Research standards

Each episode begins with a question, not a predetermined conclusion. Search for original studies and credible reviews, including evidence that challenges the proposed narrative. Record search terms, dates, inclusion decisions and unresolved gaps where practical. This is an editorial evidence review, not a systematic review unless a documented protocol justifies that label.

- Record authors, year, title, venue, DOI or stable URL, and inspection scope (abstract or full text).
- Check design, sample, comparison, outcomes, limitations and conflicting evidence.
- Distinguish observation, association, experimental evidence, interpretation and speculation.
- Map consequential claims to sources; check numbers and quotations against originals. Revise or remove unsupported claims.
- Avoid generalising laboratory findings without evidence or implying causation from association.
- Verify references against final narration before release; AI assistance does not replace source checking.
- Label demonstrations as illustrations, not audience experiments or validated measures. Do not infer individual mental states or collect identifiable viewer data as research.

## Publication gate
Confirm the corresponding video is public before releasing the episode record. Archive claims, references, limitations and any creator-approved transcript. Preserve uncertainties and document corrections. Do not upload copyrighted papers, private scripts or personal data.
